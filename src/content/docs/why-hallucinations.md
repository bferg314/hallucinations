---
title: "Why \"Hallucinations\"?"
---

The writing in this collection was produced by various AI models over the course of several years. I directed, prompted, and shaped each piece, sometimes loosely, sometimes with heavy involvement at every step, but I didn't write any of it in the traditional sense. "Commissions" describes that relationship: a commissioner provides the intent, the vision, and often detailed feedback throughout the process, while the execution belongs to someone (or something) else. It's a word that has always covered the full spectrum from "surprise me" to "I need exactly this," and it doesn't misrepresent anyone's contribution. These are things I caused to exist, not things I wrote.

So why call the collection *Hallucinations*?

When an AI model produces something untethered from its source material, we call it a hallucination. It's meant as a criticism, but it's also just a description of how these models work all the time: they generate a plausible reality from patterns, and sometimes we like the result and call it writing, and sometimes we don't and call it an error. The mechanism is the same either way. Everything here is a hallucination in that sense — generated, not retrieved — and I mean that as a feature.

But the deeper reason is that hallucinating is not something machines started doing. It's what brains have always done. Your experience of this moment isn't a raw feed from the world; it's your brain's best running guess, a prediction assembled from expectations and checked loosely against the senses. Neuroscientists have taken to calling perception a "controlled hallucination," where the control comes from reality's constant corrections. Color isn't out there. Neither is sound as you experience it, or the feeling that time flows, or the sense that there's a solid, continuous you at the center of it all. The world serves up wavelengths and pressure changes; the vivid, seamless show is generated in-house. When we agree about what we're hallucinating, we call it reality.

Seen that way, these pieces are hallucinations twice over: a machine's generated guesses, prompted into shape by a mind that is itself generating its reality moment to moment, now rendered into your own controlled hallucination as you read them. It's guesses all the way down, and it always has been.

It's also worth acknowledging what these models are. A massive body of human thought and expression got compressed into something I can have a working relationship with, and I pay for access to that relationship. That's the material the commission is made from: the gathered and embodied output of countless human minds. The ideas and direction are mine. The execution draws on all of humanity, and these commissions are something new in the pool. It's up to you to decide whether it's a Baby Ruth or something else... But I enjoy them, otherwise why would I share them?
